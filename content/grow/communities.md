Communities are where your users ask questions, compare tools and complain about workarounds. Being there, helpfully, is one of the best ways to find early users and learn what they need. Treat each community as someone else's home: follow its rules, give more than you take.

## Earn the right to mention your product

- **Read before you post.** Read the rules, the pinned posts and a week of threads. Many communities allow promotion only in a weekly thread, or not at all.
- **Answer questions in full.** Give the actual answer in the comment, not "DM me" or a link. If your product helps, mention it after the answer, and say it is yours.
- **Share lessons, not launches.** "What I learned getting my first 50 customers" with real numbers gets far more goodwill than "I built X, check it out".
- **One account, your real identity.** Never use extra accounts to upvote or to pretend to be a happy user. It is detected and gets products banned.

## Reddit specifics

- New accounts with little karma are often filtered automatically. Build a normal history before you need to post.
- Some subreddits rank highly in Google for their topic, so a genuinely useful thread can send visitors for years.
- Look for subreddits about the problem, the profession or the tools your users use, not only startup subreddits.
- If a moderator removes a post, read why, adjust, and do not repost the same thing elsewhere straight away.

## Discord, Slack and forums

Many niches have active Discord servers, Slack groups and old-style forums. They are smaller but more engaged. Introduce yourself in the welcome channel, help in the support channels, and share your product only in the channel meant for it.

## Running your own community

Once users ask for a place to share tips, a community can reduce support load and surface ideas. Keep it simple:

- **Choose the platform your users already use**: a subreddit, a Discord server, or [[tool:github-discussions]] for developer tools.
- **Write short rules** and pin them: be respectful, stay on topic, no spam or excessive self-promotion, follow the platform's own rules.
- **Turn on basic safety filters.** On Reddit, AutoModerator can filter posts from very new or low-karma accounts, posts containing link shorteners, and common spam phrases, while approving posts that link to your own domain. Start strict and relax as you see what real members need.
- **Show up every day for the first months**: answer posts, thank contributors, share updates.
- **Invite your best users to help moderate** once the community grows.
