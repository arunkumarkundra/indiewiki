Content is the slow channel that keeps working while you sleep. A page written today can bring visitors for years. The catch: it takes months to start, and search engines and readers both ignore generic pages. Write fewer, better pages, starting closest to what people search when they are ready to act.

The technical groundwork is in the [SEO checklist](/ship/seo-checklist/). This page is about what to write.

## Write in this order

1. **Feature and use-case pages.** One page per main job your product does, in the words customers use: "Send invoices from WhatsApp", not "Messaging integrations".
2. **Comparison and alternative pages.** "[Competitor] alternatives" and "[You] vs [Competitor]". Be honest about where the competitor is better. People trust balanced pages and stay longer.
3. **Answers to support questions.** Every question that reaches your inbox twice deserves a page.
4. **Guides for the problem around your product.** The tasks your users do before and after using your product.
5. **Original data and tools.** Small free calculators, templates, or numbers from your own product. These attract links naturally.

## Find what people search for

- **Ask your users** what they typed into Google before they found you.
- **Search Console** shows the queries you already appear for. Pages with many impressions are your best leads. See [Google Search Console](/ship/google-search-console/).
- **Autocomplete and "People also ask"** on Google show the exact phrasing of related questions.
- **Communities**: the titles of popular threads in your niche are search queries in disguise.
- **Keyword tools** help estimate volume. Look for specific, lower-competition phrases rather than broad, high-volume ones you cannot win yet.

## Make each page the best answer

- Answer the question in the first two sentences.
- Add what only you can: screenshots, real numbers, mistakes you made, examples from customers.
- Use headings that mirror the questions people ask.
- Link to related pages on your site, and from them back to this one.
- Show who wrote it and when it was last updated.
- Update the page when facts change, and only then change the date.

## Using AI without publishing filler

AI is useful for outlines, first drafts, rewording and checking that a page covers the obvious sub-questions. It is weak at the things that make a page worth ranking: experience, specific examples and opinion. A practical workflow:

1. Write the outline and your key points yourself, from real experience.
2. Let AI draft around your points.
3. Cut every generic sentence: warm-up intros, repeated summaries, vague claims.
4. Add screenshots, numbers and examples.
5. Read it out loud. If it sounds like nobody in particular wrote it, rewrite it.

## Group pages into clusters

Choose a few topics that matter to your product. For each, write one broad guide (the pillar) and several narrow pages that link to it and to each other. This tells search engines what you are an authority on, and helps readers find the next page they need.

## Be patient, then prune

New sites often see little search traffic for three to six months. Keep publishing steadily. After six months, look at which pages bring visitors and sign-ups, improve those, and merge or remove pages that bring nothing and overlap with others.
