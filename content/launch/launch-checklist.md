A launch is a week of focused attention, not one post. The work that decides how it goes happens before the day: a page that explains the product in five seconds, a list of people to tell, and visuals ready to share. Use this list to prepare, then tick through the day itself.

## Two weeks before

- [ ] The product works end to end for a new user, on desktop and phone, including sign-up, the main task and payment.
- [ ] The [website essentials](/ship/website-essentials/), [SEO](/ship/seo-checklist/) and [security](/ship/security-checklist/) checklists are done.
- [ ] Error tracking and an uptime alarm are on. See [Monitoring and backups](/run/monitoring-and-backups/).
- [ ] The landing page says what it is, who it is for and what to do next, above the fold, in the words customers use.
- [ ] Screenshots, a 30 to 90 second demo video and a short GIF are ready. See [Screenshots and demos](/launch/screenshots-and-demos/).
- [ ] You have picked two or three launch sites that fit your audience. See [Where to launch](/launch/where-to-launch/).
- [ ] You have a list of everyone who should hear first: waitlist, interviewees, beta users, friends who would genuinely care.
- [ ] Analytics shows sign-ups and where they come from, so you can tell which launch channel worked.
- [ ] You have a way to collect emails from visitors who are not ready to sign up.

## A few days before

- [ ] Launch posts are drafted for each site, adapted to its culture (plain and technical for Hacker News, visual for Product Hunt, story-led for Indie Hackers and Reddit).
- [ ] A short personal email to your list is written, with one clear link.
- [ ] A first comment is ready that explains why you built it and asks for specific feedback.
- [ ] You have cleared the launch day of other work, and planned for your time zone and your audience's.
- [ ] Any launch-only offer is set up and tested (a discount code works, the price page shows it).
- [ ] Support is ready: chat or email answered fast, a short FAQ for the obvious questions.

## Launch day

- [ ] Post on your first launch site early in its day.
- [ ] Email your list and message the people who helped, asking for honest feedback, not upvotes.
- [ ] Share on your own social accounts with the demo video or GIF.
- [ ] Reply to every comment and question within the hour, thoughtfully.
- [ ] Watch errors and uptime; fix anything breaking new users first.
- [ ] Write down every piece of feedback and every feature request in one place.

## The week after

- [ ] Post on the next launch site or community, a day or two apart, not all at once.
- [ ] Thank everyone who tried it, and personally email each new sign-up with one question about why they came.
- [ ] Ship one or two quick fixes from feedback and tell the people who asked.
- [ ] Write a short "what I learned launching" post. These often travel further than the launch itself.
- [ ] Review the numbers: which channel sent sign-ups that stayed, not just visits.
- [ ] Add the product to relevant directories and comparison sites for steady long-term traffic.
