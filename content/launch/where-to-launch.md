Different launch sites reach different people. Pick the two or three where your actual users spend time, and do them well. Each entry links to the Toolbox for details; the full list is in [Places to launch](/tools/launch-platforms/).

## The big three

**[[tool:product-hunt]]** ranks new products every day. The audience is makers, early adopters and investors. Visual products with a clear demo do best. Prepare a tagline under 60 characters, a gallery of screenshots, a short video, and a first comment explaining why you made it. Engage all day. A top-five finish brings a burst of traffic and a permanent page; a lower finish still brings feedback and a backlink.

**[[tool:show-hn]]** on Hacker News reaches a technical, critical audience. Title it "Show HN: [Name] – [what it does, plainly]". Link to something people can try right now, not a waitlist. Write the first comment yourself: what it is, why you built it, how it works technically, and what you want feedback on. Answer criticism calmly and specifically.

**[[tool:indie-hackers]]** is for builders sharing progress, numbers and lessons. A launch post that tells the story honestly (what you tried, what failed, early numbers) does better than an announcement.

## Communities where your users already are

**[[tool:reddit]]** can be your best channel or get you banned. Each subreddit sets its own rules, and many forbid self-promotion outside specific threads. Read the rules and recent posts, take part for a while first, then share something genuinely useful (a lesson, a free resource) with your product as context, not as the headline. Good threads rank in Google for years.

Also consider Discord and Slack groups in your niche, LinkedIn for B2B, X and Bluesky for tech audiences, and specialist forums. See [Communities and Reddit](/grow/communities/).

## Directories and smaller launch sites

These bring steady trickles of visitors and backlinks:

- [[tool:betalist]] for early adopters who like trying new startups.
- [[tool:peerlist]] Launchpad for developer-built products, on a weekly cycle.
- [[tool:devhunt]] for developer tools specifically.
- [[tool:alternativeto]] for products that replace well-known paid software.
- Niche directories in your category: search "[your category] directory" and "[competitor] alternatives".

## For games

Post playable builds on itch.io, join game jams, and share short gameplay clips on social video. Open a Steam page early if you will sell there, so wishlists can build up before release.

## A sensible order

1. Your own list and the people you interviewed.
2. One niche community where your users are.
3. Product Hunt or Show HN, whichever fits your audience.
4. Indie Hackers and relevant subreddits, with a story-led post.
5. Directories, over the following weeks.

Track sign-ups by source for each step. After the first month you will know which one or two channels to keep investing in.
