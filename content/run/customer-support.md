In the early days, support is not a cost. It is the clearest research you will ever get: real users telling you, in their own words, where the product fails them. Make it easy to reach you, reply quickly, and keep a list of what you hear.

## Make contact easy

- A chat widget or contact link on every page of the app. [[tool:crisp]] has a free plan and mobile apps.
- A contact page with an email address on your domain.
- A short feedback form for ideas and bugs. [[tool:tally]] is free and fast to build.
- Your hours and expected reply time, stated plainly.

## Reply well, quickly

- **Answer the question first**, then add context.
- **Use the customer's words** and confirm you understood.
- **Say what you will do and when**, and then do it.
- **Fix small things fast** and tell the person. "Fixed it, thanks to you" turns users into fans.
- **Save good answers** as reusable replies, but always personalise the first line.

## Turn conversations into product decisions

Keep one simple list (a spreadsheet or a board) of every request and complaint, with how many people raised it. Review it weekly. Build what many people need, not what the loudest person wants.

Questions that come up twice deserve a help page. Questions that come up five times deserve a product change so nobody needs to ask.

## Protect your time

- Batch email support into one or two blocks a day, except for urgent issues.
- Write a public FAQ and link it in auto-replies.
- Set office hours in the chat widget so people know when to expect a reply.
- Decide in advance what you will refund without discussion, and do it gracefully.

## When customers are upset

Thank them for telling you, acknowledge the problem plainly, fix what you can, and do not argue about who is right. A well-handled complaint often creates a more loyal customer than one who never had a problem.
