## Make the promise specific and supportable

Describe who the offer is for, what work or outcome it covers, what the customer must provide, what is excluded, how long delivery takes, and what it costs. Explain uncertainty and dependencies. Avoid claims such as guaranteed income, effortless transformation, or universal results unless you can substantiate them and the terms are clear.

The supplied offer-advice image is a practitioner-oriented prompt. It contains assertive persuasion claims but does not provide enough evidence to treat a particular offer formula as objectively superior. Use it to generate hypotheses about clarity, risk, and perceived value; test those with real buyers.

## Test comprehension, not enthusiasm

Ask a prospective customer to explain what they believe they would receive, for whom it is intended, and what happens after purchase. Confusion is a signal to clarify scope. A purchase, deposit, or scheduled implementation is stronger behavior evidence than a compliment, but still does not prove repeatable demand.

State refund, cancellation, and support terms before payment. Ensure the price and promises comply with applicable consumer and advertising requirements. Review your own delivery cost so a successful sale can be fulfilled sustainably.
